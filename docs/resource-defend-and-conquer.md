# Resource, defend, and conquer

The silhouetted characters stay. They are simpler to implement, and they read the way Warcraft II units read. This note is for describing a merge of Stick War and Warcraft II, not for shipping Stick War.

## Keep

Silhouettes. A short match. A clear thing to destroy. Miners. A handful of unit roles you can see at a glance.

## From Stick War

Gold comes off patches that miners work. A second resource sits beside gold and is spent on the stronger options. You train units until a population cap stops you. You can take a few units by hand instead of only ordering the group. The match ends when the enemy statue falls, or when someone holds the center long enough that the other side cannot.

## From Warcraft II

You build a base, you do not only queue troops from a deck. Two resources, gold and lumber, are hauled by peons. Farms are supply: you cannot field more army than the farms allow. Production buildings gate which unit types exist, so the roster stays small and tied to what you have built. Fog of war hides the map until you walk into it. You select the army and move it as a group across a map, and the usual way the match ends is destroying the enemy base. Exact costs and the full building list are not restated here.

## The merge

Stick-figure units on a Warcraft II-shaped map: a base, two resources, supply, a few production buildings, fog, and an army you move as a group. The silhouettes stay so each role is readable at a glance, and a few of them can still be driven by hand the way Stick War lets you drive one unit. What is still open is whether the map is one base to attack or a lane with a center to hold, whether the thing you kill is a statue or a town hall, and whether spells and pre-match decks stay at all.

## Open

- One base on a map, or a lane with a center to hold.
- Statue or town hall as the thing you destroy.
- Whether a second resource is lumber, crystals, or something else.
- Whether supply is farms, a population cap, or both.
- Whether you build production buildings, or pick a deck before the match.
- Whether spells stay.
- Whether you still drive a few units by hand.
- How long a match should be.
